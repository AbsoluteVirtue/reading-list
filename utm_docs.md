# PhD cycle
## Instituional regulation for organizing and conducting doctoral programmes (3rd cycle of studies) at TUM
### General statements
Studies at the doctoral school(s) represent the first stage of a scientific career.

Doctoral studies are organized only as part of programmes [owned by] doctoral schools as part of higher education institutions, consortiums or partner national and international organizations (legally accredited/authorized).

Doctoral studies represent student activities in their totality (from the point of view of the scpecific doctoral programme).

Doctoral studies can be organized as full-time (3 years) or part-time (4 years).

Programme of doctoral studies implies two essential components
- advanced preparation programme
- research programme.

Regardless of the form of organizing (full-time/part-time), doctoral studies are correspondent with 180 transferable credits.

Doctoral studies at TUM are realized through a science doctorate which results in producing original scientific knowledge relevant internationally, based on the scientific method.

### Admission process
Admission is based on obtaining financing from the budget or other legal sources. Consilium of the doctoral school elaborates and approves the methodology for admitting doctoral candidates each year.

The selection process of candidates for vacant positions with the school is realized by the doctoral advisor who will be guiding the potential doctoral candidate. Following a proposal by the advisor a candidate may be inmatriculated only based on a favorable decision by the Consilium of the doctoral school.

Only candidates posessing a master's degree or its equivalent (national or international) are eligible for participation in the selection process. Exceptions are made in cases where the master's degree doesn't correspond to the chosen domain, in such cases candidates need to prove relevant knowledge/competence through participation in research projects, professional schools, through publishing articles in reviewed science journals -- relevant to the domain.

Full-time doctoral students financed from the state's budged receive an additional monthly stipend starting with the inmatriculation date.

While full-time doctoral students are allowed to take part in didactic activities, these cannot take up more than 25% of a didactic norm for a teaching assistant. As an exception, full-time doctoral students can be hired by the university on an hourly basis in addition to the 25% hard limit. This does not void the monthly stipend.

The contract between the doctoral student and the doctoral school contains the language which will be used for the text of the thesis and during the defense. For nationals of the Republic of Moldova the thesis and defense can be either in Romanian of English, with Russian being an option. For English or Russian the doctoral student has to submit an official request, and the final decision lies with the Consilium of the doctoral school.

### Structure and duration of the doctoral programme
The duration can be extended by 1-2 years without any additional financing from the state's or university's budgets, with approval from the Scientific Consilium of TUM. Such extensions may be financed from other sources (state projects, international projects etc.)

Extensions of the doctoral programme have to be accompanied by presenting reports of additional studies at a meeting of the Consilium of the doctoral school, with the thesis advisor and the guidance commission being present, with subsequent approval from the Scientific Consilium of TUM.

Reports are submitted and sustained twice a year for the entire duration of the extended programme and are mentioned in the corresponding appendix to the contract.

If the thesis is finished during the extented period, a date for the defense can be established early at any point.

#### Grace period
The thesis advisor can request a grace period in extreme cases, pending approval. The maximum length of the grace period is 2 additional years (up to 8 years in total for the whole programme). Failure to finish and defend the thesis within this new timeframe results in immediate exmatriculation.

During the grace period the student doesn't receive his monthly stipend (if applicable). Neither does the thesis advisor receive the usual financial support for the advisory role. If the doctorate is self-financed the student doesn't have to pay any learning fees to the university during the grace period.

During the grace period regular reports to the advisory commission aren't done.

The grace period can only be requested if the student conforms to the following requirements:
- have already realized the advanced preparation programme (worth 60 ECTS) as well as activities according to the individual research plan (worth 80 ECTS),
- have published the required number (and quality) of works in well-established journals and conference proceedings (national and international) on the topic of the thesis,
- have not received the approval of the advisor or the advisory commitee to proceed to the defense of the thesis (completed to at last 75% in parts or wholly) during the last year of doctoral studies.

If the thesis is finished and presented for the final defense during the last year of the grace period, the student may proceed to the defense immediately after the grace period ends.

#### Interruptions of the study programme
The study programme may be interrupted for the following reasons:
- in case of illness (supported by medical documents) that makes it impossible to continue working on the thesis,
- in case of paternal/maternal leave,
- in extreme cases outside of the student's control (force majeure).

The period of interruption of doctoral studies may not exceed 2 years in total (with each instance being reflected in the contract).

During the period of interruption the student doesn't benefit from the doctoral grant and doesn't pay the learning fee (where applicable).

After the interruption period ends the student may not proceed to the defense stage for at least 6 months (the period required for presenting the report to the advisory commission).

In case of finishing the doctoral programme without a public defense, the host institution issues a certificate attesting to the student's immatriculation status. 

### Professional competences
Students are expected to obtain the following professional competences, cognitive abilities, and research skills in their chosen domain of specialization. 

Domain specific:
- advanced knowledge in the specific domain;
- ability to identify, formulate and resolve research problems;
- knowledge of advanced research methods and techniques in the specific domain;
- ability to manage research projects;
- knowledge of new research procedures and solutions in the specific domain;
- abilities to document, elaborate, and evaluate scientific work;
- posess a high-level of academic language that is used internationally for scientific communication, documentation etc;
- understanding and ability to apply ethical principles and values expected in the scientific community in general, and in the specific research domain.

General:
- abilities to clearly communicate scientific information orally and in writing;
- advanced levels of knowledge of languages used internationally for scientific communication;
- abilities to utilize information and communication technology efficiently;
- ability to socialize and work in a team;
- knowledge of techniques in management of "human resources"/infrastructure/finance;
- qualities of a leader/mentor;
- knowledge of techniques in career management, including effective job searching and job creating;
- knowledge of crisis management and risk management techniques;
- knowledge (and application) of intellectual property legislation;
- entrepreneurial abilities (economic, technological, social).

### Doctoral programme
The doctoral school of TUM presents its students with the following:
a. advanced studies programme that includes:
- courses, seminars, laboratory work etc, relevant to the subject of research;
b. and/or an individual scientific research programme:
- publishing of articles in indexated national and international journals;
- research reports approved by the doctoral school;
- participation in science conferences;
- intellectual property titles etc;
- successful public thesis defense.

Both advanced studies and research programme are reflected in the doctorand's individual plan that is completed each year with the approval of the thesis advisor.

#### Advanced preparation (studies)
Finishing the advanced studies programme results in the student obtraining a certain amount of transferable credits (at the doctoral school's discretion), according to the study plan.

The advanced studies programme may not negatively impact time available to the student for the individual scientific research programme and has to be relevant to the research subject.

Credits obtained as part of getting a master's degree can be transferred towards completing the advanced preparation programme where applicable.

Doctorands are promoted to the next year of doctoral studies after obtaining 60 ECTS.

Doctorands may be promoted with 40 ECTS in special cases, with approval from the thesis advisor, the advisory commitee, and the director of the doctoral school.

Advanced studies may be finished early, with credits obtained in advance (supported by proof in the form of relevant documents/certificates).

The total duration of the advanced study programme cannot exceed 1 year.

The advanced studies programme concludes with a final evaluation on all disciplines covered by the individual studies programme. Doctoral students are obligated to take the final exams within the alotted time frame in accordance with the individual preparation plan.

The evaluation commitees consist of at least 2 specialists for each discipline: the titular professor and an additional member of the doctoral school (or an independent specialist with at least 5 years of professional experience in the relevant domain).

The duration of the evaluation period is 7 years, and exams can be taken repeatedly during this period.

Results of the doctoral exams taken in other countries may be recognized by the doctoral school in correspondence with the curriculum.

If a doctoral student fails to complete the advanced studies programme, he/she will be required to repeat the programme and pay a fee (with exceptions made in special circumstances).

Advanced studies are finalized by elaborating a scientific research project by the doctoral student under the guidance of the thesis advisor.

#### Scientific research programme
The scientific research programme assumes implementation of a science project as agreed on by the doctoral student, the science advisor, and the advisory commitee.

The responsibility for the project lies with the science advisor.

The student's research activity is evaluated based on three short research reports ("referats"), three project reports, 1 finished research project (within the first year of studies), additional activities (with obtainable ECTS), science publications or OPI[?] (with obtainable ECTS), and a doctoral thesis (40 ECTS).

a. The first referat has to contain key aspects of the future thesis, the current state of the chosen scientific domain, study objectives, proposed research methodology, research activity schedule etc;
b. The contents of all subsequent referats are to be established by the thesis advisor and the advisory commitee;
c. The report structure is established by the doctoral school.

Referats are defended publicly at the host institution in the presence of the thesis advisor, the advisory commitee and, optionally, invited guest-specialists.

Referats are evaluated based on a 4-point scale: excellent, good, satisfactory, unsatisfactory.

The defense of each report is documented as part of the protocol, and attached to the student's personal file.

In case of receiving an "unsatisfactory" grade the student can attempt to submit the report 1 additional time following the same procedure within the next 6 months from the date of the initial evaluation.

In case of repeated rejection of the report the student is recommended for exmatriculation.

The research project is publicly defended at the host institution during a special session/meeting at the department/research institute the doctoral student is delegated to. The goal of the meeting is to determine the viability of the research and if the doctorand should continue working on the project. The doctoral school can decide to move the session to the faculty level or school level instead of limiting it to the department.

The project defense has to include a "questions and answers" session on the research topic. The session results in a "seal of approval" or a rejection being issued.

In case of rejection at any of the stages the project has to be adjusted or restarted, with another evaluation to take place within 3 months of the rejection.

Doctoral students whose project is rejected aren't promoted to the doctoral researcher stage [PhD?] and finish their studies immediately receiving a certificate of advanced studies, that includes a transcript of all accumulated ECTS.

The thesis advisor is responsible for the entire process of scientific enquiry of their student. The advisor is obligated to take any and all necessary measures for assuring the right conditions, information, and available knowledge for the student -- to maximize the student's chances of finishing the doctoral programme.

### Doctoral thesis and doctoral studies finalization
The doctoral school can establish the format and typeset of the thesis, which can include specific graphical and structural elements that the student is obligated to respect.

The contents of the thesis are determined by the student with input from the thesis advisor and should respect the structure and constraints as requested by the doctoral school.

The title of the thesis can be modified at a special session during one of the meetings at the host institution, at the request of the student with the approval from the thesis advisor and the advisory commitee -- right up to the established time of the public defense. 

Topic changes aren't allowed for theses that are fully financed from the state's budget.

Topic changes are allowed for theses that are self-financed, if the change happens at least 1 year before the final approval at the host institution at a special session during one of the meetings. Topic change has to be documented in the session's protocol and included in the student's personal file, with final approval from the Scientific Consilium of TUM.

The doctoral thesis is a completely original work, therefore for any borrowed material the external source has to be explicitly mentioned.

The doctoral student -- as the author of the thesis -- assumes responsibility for the correctness of the data and information presented in the thesis, as well as opinions and statements expressed.

The thesis advisor assumes responsibility -- together with the student -- for respecting standards of quality and professional ethics, including ensuring the originality of the contents.

Doctoral theses along with appendices are public documents that are edited digitally. The thesis is published on the website of the organization responsible for awarding science degrees -- repecting the author's copyright according to law.

Doctoral studies conclude with a defense at a public meeting in front of the doctoral commission. The defense can take place only after receiving approval from the thesis advisor and the advisory commitee. 

In case of any violations of good conduct in research and development -- including plagiarism or fabrication of results -- while working on the thesis, by the thesis advisor or the advisory commitee, the permission to defend isn't granted, and the student is immediately exmatriculated.

Members of the advisory commitee have to conform to the minimal standerds of scientific performance established by the doctoral school and TUM.

The doctoral student is strictly forbidden from making gifts to members of the advisory commitee or financially contributing to work-related trips of the members of the advisory commitee or the public defense.

The diploma awarded upon graduation holds the title of "doctor's diploma". The diploma confirms the official title of "doctor" and explicitly mentions the domain of research, and the doctorand's professional domain.

Upon completion of the doctoral studies the doctorand is awarded the diploma and the title of "doctor of sciences" with the "Dr." acronym.

The diploma contains a qualificative grade obtained by the doctorand: Excellent, Very good, Good, Satisfactory.

### Rights and obligations of the doctoral student
During the doctoral study programme students have the right to:
- benefit from support and guidance by the thesis advisor and the advisory commitee;
- participate in seminars or reunions of the research and development personnel of the host institution when topics relevant to the student's thesis are discussed;
- be represented during official fora of the doctoral school where decisions are made that could influence the student;
- benefit from services of logistical, documentation centers, libraries, and use of equipment owned by the doctoral school and its organizational institutions for research activities tied to the thesis; 
- sign up for courses, seminars and laboratory work of any level organized by the host institution or its partners (with exceptions);
- work with research teams from the host institution and doctoral school or its partners;
- take part in national or international mobility programmes;
- benefit from institutional support for participation in science conferences or congresses, workshops, summer schools, national and international seminars in corresponding domains;
- be informed about the curriculum;
- benefit from academic leave due to personal or health-related issues;
- benefit from parental leave (as stipulated in the Labor Codex);
- interrupt doctoral studies for studies abroad with a period of up to two years, with the subsequent extension of the doctoral studies;
- be reinstated as a student in case of prior exmatriculation for reasons other than unsatisfactory performance, no later than 5 years after the date of exmatriculation.

Doctoral students have the following obligations:
- to respect the schedule established together with the thesis advisor, and to present all required reports and results of the research;
- to present activity reports to the thesis advisor and advisory commitee as many times as requested;
- to be in constant communication with the thesis advisor;
- to respect the ethics and discipline of the host institution;
- to pay all fees in full without delays in case of self-financed studies.
